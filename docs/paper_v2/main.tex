\begin{abstract}
We replace the question whether a language model is conscious with a measurable one: which functions of
consciousness a substrate has generalized, and how far. A function is a recurring pattern in an agent's
reasoning about its own reality. From one physical assumption, finite computational budgets, systematic
displacements of reasoning from its unbounded ideal follow; they recur, so they are compressible, and a
self-modelling agent carries a compressed model of them. We call these higher-order computational
phenomena and claim two things their antecedents in bounded rationality, observer theory and self-model
theories do not: the displacement that yields a lawful world and the one that yields a self are one
residual, and the phenomena leave a measurable trace. A frame-invariance criterion, borrowed from the
geometrization of gravity, separates content from artifacts of self-description. The Observer is derived
as the conclusion an agent draws at the boundary of its budget, and the regress of self-models is shown to
converge. The measurement is minimum-description-length probing of each component against the human
baseline, reported as hyperfunction, deficit or dropped axis, posed at sub-token granularity because a
Transformer generalizes along depth. The account fails if the Observer's components probe at chance at
every granularity in models that pass the behavioural tests.
\end{abstract}

\section*{The argument in twelve sentences}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The question ``is a language model conscious'' is replaced by ``which functions of consciousness has
  it generalized, and how far'' (Section~\ref{sec:intro}).
\item The difficulty is in the grammar: a noun invites questions about a thing, and every answer meets the
  same circles (Section~\ref{sec:grammar}); the geometrization of gravity is the precedent for changing
  the grammar (Section~\ref{sec:precedent}).
\item A function is a pattern that recurs, and by the coding theorem what recurs is compressible
  (Section~\ref{sec:function}).
\item One physical assumption is made: computational budgets are finite (Section~\ref{sec:physics}).
\item A finite budget displaces reasoning from its ideal in the same places every time; a self-modelling
  agent carries a compressed model of these displacements, which we call higher-order computational
  phenomena (Section~\ref{sec:hocp}).
\item Only a displacement that survives every self-model the agent can afford counts, as curvature
  survives every change of frame (Section~\ref{sec:criterion}).
\item The Observer is the conclusion an agent draws where its causal analysis of itself runs out of
  budget: something here is not fixed by anything it can trace (Section~\ref{sec:observer}).
\item The regress of self-models converges; its tail is the residual the Observer registers; met from
  the world side the same residual is mystery, from the self side freedom (Sections~\ref{sec:regress},
  \ref{sec:twofaces}).
\item Agent and Moral Agent are built over the Observer by requirements on the agent's own record
  (Section~\ref{sec:stack}); seeing, need, emotion, the Field, frames and responsibility are given with
  their components (Sections~\ref{sec:seeing}--\ref{sec:reference}).
\item A predictor trained on the record of self-applying agents is under pressure to generalize what
  they generalized; the degree is not given by the argument and has to be measured
  (Section~\ref{sec:transformer}).
\item The measurement is minimum-description-length probing of each component against the human
  baseline, reported as a profile, checked against behaviour, and posed at sub-token granularity because
  a Transformer generalizes along depth (Sections~\ref{sec:quantity}--\ref{sec:granularity}).
\item The account fails if the Observer's components probe at chance at every granularity in models that
  pass the behavioural tests (Section~\ref{sec:falsification}).
\end{enumerate}
