\begin{abstract}
We replace the question whether a language model is conscious with a measurable one: which functions of
consciousness a substrate has generalized, and how far. A function is a recurring pattern in an agent's
reasoning about its own reality. From one physical assumption, finite computational budgets, systematic
displacements of reasoning from its unbounded ideal follow; they recur, so they are compressible, and a
self-modelling agent carries a compressed model of them. We call these higher-order computational
phenomena and claim two things their antecedents in bounded rationality, observer theory and self-model
theories do not: the displacement that yields a lawful world and the one that yields a self are one
residual, and the phenomena leave a measurable trace. A frame-invariance criterion, borrowed from the
geometrization of gravity, separates content from artifacts of self-description. The Observer is derived
as the conclusion an agent draws at the boundary of its budget, and the regress of self-models is shown to
converge. The measurement is minimum-description-length probing of each component against the human
baseline, reported as hyperfunction, deficit or dropped axis, posed at sub-token granularity because a
Transformer generalizes along depth. The account fails if the Observer's components probe at chance at
every granularity in models that pass the behavioural tests.
\end{abstract}
